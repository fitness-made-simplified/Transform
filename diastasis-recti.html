Why It Happens

It's Rarely "Just Fat"

Four things usually combine to create the pooch. Training through them without addressing the structure is why generic ab workouts fail postpartum clients.
01

Diastasis Recti

Linea alba separation. Present in 60–70% of women at 6 weeks postpartum; 30–40% still unresolved at 12 months without targeted rehab.

02

Scar Adhesion

The C-section scar can bind to fascia and muscle beneath it, restricting core function and creating the visible "shelf."

03

Pelvic Floor Dysfunction

Almost always co-occurs with DR. Loading the core without addressing this raises prolapse risk.

04

Subcutaneous Fat

Real, and it does need a deficit to shift — but fat loss alone won't correct the shape if the structure underneath is still open.

The Protocol

4-Phase Recovery Timeline

Tap a phase to expand. This order is deliberate — skipping ahead is the most common reason recovery stalls.
1
Clearance & Assessment
Week 0–6 (per doctor clearance)
▾
  • No structured exercise until medical clearance — C-section clearance often runs longer than vaginal delivery due to layered incision healing.
  • Self-check for DR gap width and tissue tension (see tool above).
  • Check scar mobility once fully closed: is it mobile in all directions, or tethered and numb?
    • 2
    Foundation Rebuild
    Week 6–12
    ▾
  • Diaphragmatic breathing — 360° breathing to reconnect diaphragm, TVA, and pelvic floor.
  • Scar massage & mobilization once cleared — 2–3 min daily desensitization.
  • TVA activation — heel slides, regressed dead bugs, bird-dog. No crunching pattern yet.
  • Pelvic floor coordination — exhale-engage pattern, not isolated Kegels.
    • 3
    Progressive Loading
    Month 3–6
    ▾
  • Anti-rotation / anti-extension core work — Pallof press, plank progressions — only if no doming under load.
  • Full-body compound strength training drives more fat loss and postural correction than isolated ab work.
  • Caloric deficit only once healed, if fat loss is a goal: 300–500 kcal deficit, 1.6–2g/kg protein to preserve muscle.
    • 4
    Aesthetics Layer
    Month 6+
    ▾
  • Once core function is restored and DR is closed or functional, standard hypertrophy/fat-loss programming applies.
  • If the gap remains 2–3+ finger-widths with poor tension after 12 months of proper rehab, refer to a pelvic floor PT — surgical repair is a legitimate outcome for some, not a failure.

    Myth vs. Reality

    Myth
    Reality
    "Just do more crunches"
    Can worsen DR and raise hernia risk.
    "Waist trainers close the gap"
    No evidence for it — may increase intra-abdominal pressure and worsen DR.
    "It's just fat, diet harder"
    Ignores the structural issue. Many women in a deficit still keep the pooch.
    "6 weeks and you're fully healed"
    Fascia and scar remodeling takes 6–12+ months.
